## Name: Silky (Mervin) Bitterbottom
## Species:  Gnome
## Class: Warlock - 
## Pronouns: (He/him)
## Description:
After the unjust execution of his parents by the magistrate, Mofongo, of the Woodlands, Mervin Bitterbottom, a young gnome, was orphaned and destitute, left with only his wits and a simple wooden engraving of his parents faces and names.

After many years sleeping in stables, sheds, and haylofts, Mervin had grown older, but not much taller nor wiser. He found a permanent shelter in the attic crawlspace of the Woodland library, accessible only to beings one-quarter the size of typical Woodland beings. He lived there rent-free, unknown to anyone in the Woodlands. Alas, he had a space to call home and free books to read, but it was space enough for only one, and he missed his parents dearly. 

To keep himself fed and clothed, Mervin frequented the corner tavern every day to pilfer hard-earned coin from the regulars. He would run card games, shuffle shells, and tell long, expansive tales. Mervin was part of the atmosphere and entertainment for the regulars. The barkeep didn’t mind because Mervin wasn’t actually proficient at duping anyone sober. He frequently lost his bets, or his epic stories would unwind under scrutiny, and so he wasn’t taken very seriously. He was teased about his name, and the regulars poked fun at his height, goading the tavern dogs to chase him around the tavern like he was a dog toy.

One night, while walking the aisles of the Woodland library after an unsuccessful day at the tavern, Mervin came across a particularly ancient-looking light blue book located on a Gnome-height shelf in the “enchanted objects” section. This book had frayed pages, gold-plated edges, a buckle, and was particularly dusty. No other books in the library looked like it, as they were elven - pristine but with drab green and brown hues. 

Mervin stole the unique book back to his crawlspace where he cracked open the buckle and rifled through the delicate pages. He landed on a page-sized image of a spiral circle. Intrigued, Mervin traced the spiral lines with his finger not once, not twice, but three times and…poof! Out leapt a small, light blue genie with the same color and gold accent.

The genie had been living within the pages of the book for hundreds of years and was quite lonely. The genie told Mervin that he had many powers. Namely, that he could inhabit inanimate objects and feel the emotion that they represent and that he could bestow special powers upon beings. And so, Mervin saw an opportunity for a deal to be made. Mervin would allow the genie to enchant the wooden engraving of his parents, finally feeling the love of others. And the genie would bestow special powers of social persuasion upon Mervin, in hopes that he could earn recompense from Mofongo for the deaths of his parents. 

After the deal was struck, Mervin’s confidence ballooned, and he quickly outgrew his usual tavern tricks, swindling more adept (read: sober) targets. He successfully convinced the shopkeeper, the cobbler, and the haberdasher to each part ways with their finest clothing and accessory articles. Mervin’s reputation grew, and he was adorned with the nickname “Silky” due to his deft tongue, convincing storytelling, and polished regalia. He preferred this name over Mervin, and so he began introducing himself as such as his reputation grew. Silky could sell water to a fish. Don’t worry, he’ll tell you about it sometime…

With his tail feathers upright and his chest puffed outwards, Silky’s ambition for retribution against the Mofongo also grew. He contrived a detailed plan to convince not only Mofongo himself, but all the commoners in the Woodlands that it was indeed he, Silky, and not the Mofongo, who was rightfully and legally the Woodland magistrate due to the unjust killing of his parents. 

His logic was impenetrable, his storytelling was illustrative and convincing, but when his case came to trial, the public attention was squarely on Silky. Devastating information came to light. An anonymous source informed the court that Silky was utilizing library resources without a library card… Silky became a pariah, a thief of public resources. With “the people” behind him, Mofongo laid judgment on Silky, giving him two options.

He could be drafted into the Queen’s war, accepting almost certain death. Or, he would be allowed to remain in the Woodlands, and his tongue would be cut from his mouth and chained around his neck, stripping him of his persuasive powers and signaling his untrustworthiness to all. 

Silky chose war with the hope of freedom in victory, retaining his powers in the hopes of avenging his parents once again.
